03 — What’s So Different About Me

Different, butspecificNot a personality. Six structural differences that change the outcome.

No translation loss

Strategy and craft sit in the same head

Most brands hire a strategist and a designer and spend the rest of the project translating between them. Here, the positioning and the design come from the same reasoning — so the idea survives all the way to the file.

Behaviour-led

I read the numbers, not just the mood

Analytics, funnel data and CTR are part of the brief, not a report that arrives after the work is done. Creative decisions get defended with behaviour, not preference.

One voice end to end

Copy is written, not filled in

Asking me for a design and getting copy in the same delivery is the point. Words and layout are designed together — the hook, the headline and the visual are one idea.

Handover-ready

Built to be used, not to be admired

Every engagement ends with usable files, templates and guidelines. The goal is a system your team can run without me — and a reason to call me back when it needs the next level.

Operator POV

I have sat on the client side of the table

Running campaigns, funnels and creator partnerships taught me what actually gets approved, what gets ignored, and which brief was wrong in the first place. That is why my first question is always about the business.

Direct contact

Small team energy, agency range

You work directly with the person doing the thinking and the making. No account layer, no hand-offs, no waiting three weeks to find out where the project is.

How I work

Four rules I do not bend

  1. Say the uncomfortable thing early

    If the brief is wrong, the sooner that gets said the less gets wasted. I would rather lose a project in week one than deliver something polished that solves the wrong problem in month three.

  2. Show the reasoning, not just the outcome

    Every decision comes with the reason behind it. That is what lets a team apply it to a situation I never saw, and it is what makes the work survive me leaving.

  3. Details are not decoration

    The caption on the ad, the crop on the cover, the second line of the email subject. These are not finishing touches. They are usually where the difference is.

  4. Craft is the argument

    A good concept executed sloppily reads as a bad concept. I care about the finish for the obvious reason — and because the finish is often the only signal a busy audience will give you.
